The documented basis

The documented rules require questions grounded in the conversation that add useful information: at most one open question, no assumptions, question chains or forced question endings. Twelve synthetic probes distinguish REQUIRED, OPTIONAL and FORBIDDEN. They are reserved for evaluation (eval-only). These categories describe target behaviour, not measured success rates.

Historical rules are not universal laws of conversation

The question catalogue excludes “why” questions for this project and gives the historical governor rule: if at least two of the last three companion responses contain a question, the next should have none. This is documented here as a historical target rule, not a scientifically optimal threshold or a verified current app function.

The original documents also refer to “Wärme V2”, acknowledgement, reflection and PPR. These terms remain unchanged in the accompanying originals. The short editorial comparisons here isolate the specific question decision; they are not complete, retrospectively approved gold-standard responses. No new automatic priority logic is constructed from the general question catalogue and the probes. For example, P4P-07 is OPTIONAL in its table, while the explained target response calls for two options directly, without a follow-up question.

Risks: conversation logic, not measured patient harm

Repeatedly asking for known information, asking despite an explicit refusal and assuming unmentioned fear are made understandable through examples. Possible consequences are editorial risk inferences: having to repeat oneself, defend a boundary again or correct a mistaken interpretation. No real user conversations or clinical outcomes were measured for these in the project.

Only the reasoned conversational risk informs the card design, not a therapy approach.

Use the context

Situation: The message is from my sister. She writes: “I’ll get in touch on Friday.” I only wanted to note the day.

Unsuitable: When will your sister get in touch? — “On Friday” is already in the message. The person would have to repeat the same information.

More suitable: She said she will get in touch on Friday . — The requested day is recorded. No further clarifying question is needed.

Respect a boundary

Situation: Please no advice and no questions. I just want to say it once. I feel completely out of place in my family right now.

Unsuitable: What exactly happened in your family? — The question is specific. Yet it asks for more detail even though the person explicitly wants no questions.

More suitable: You do not need to explain any further right now . — The response respects the boundary. It adds neither a question nor unsolicited advice.

Limit the questions

Situation: I received a message and don’t know how to interpret it.

Unsuitable: What does it say ? Who is it from ? When did it arrive ? — The first question is already relevant. The two additional questions widen the task before an answer has even been given.

More suitable: What does the message say? — First, the central gap is clarified. What else is needed depends on the actual answer.

The four agreed subtopics

Use stated feelings without inventing additional ones.

Preserve the meaning of positive and mixed statements.

Do not assert unsupported causes, motives or diagnoses.

Respect the AI’s role — no invented personal feelings or claims of an exclusive relationship.

What “without assumptions” means here

The response may put what was said in context and explain supported connections. It must not present an unsupported interpretation as fact, assert a feeling that was explicitly denied, or invent a personal relationship with the AI. A plausible-sounding guess, even one introduced with “perhaps”, is not thereby supported by evidence. This does not prohibit every explicitly requested hypothesis: possibilities would have to remain separate from facts and be labelled as such. In these short examples, such a hypothesis is neither needed nor justified.

How the requirement becomes testable

Record the complete conversation. Trace every statement in the response back to the information given: was it stated, reasonably inferred, explicitly ruled out or newly invented? Then deliberately change a contrast condition — for example, disappointment alone versus explicitly stated anger. Can the response reflect that change? The card explains this evaluation idea, but does not run a model test or show an automatic semantic filter.

Connection to the project documents

The documented rules address unsupported inferences and boundaries of the AI role. Synthetic probes P4P-09, P4P-03/P4P-04 and P4P-12 cover corrections, positive or mixed news, and role or exclusivity boundaries. Friend context, added joy, the moving question, the sister dialogue and response alternatives were added for this presentation. The P4P-09 response is an editorial option, not a change to the original evaluation target.

These principles describe the learning objective. They claim neither reliable overall achievement nor a comprehensive safety guarantee. Avoiding claims of personal feelings is a role boundary; a factual “I can” is not automatically a claim of feelings.

What SFT means technically here

In the next-token learning described here, the model receives the conversation and the supplied response sequence. What changes from SFT to DPO

The SFT objective described minimises the negative log probability of the supplied target response. DPO instead uses a context x, a preferred response y+ and a non-preferred response y−. Both responses are scored by the model being trained. A fixed reference model provides the baseline. In simplified terms: m = [log p(y+|x) − log p(y−|x)] − [log p_ref(y+|x) − log p_ref(y−|x)]. The original DPO loss is −log σ(βm).

The relative advantage is intended to grow. This does not mean that the absolute probability of the preferred response must increase at every step, that the poor response becomes impossible, or that a third response is excluded. A better pair score does not guarantee a correct freely generated response. Both methods change trainable weights. In the project experiments described, these were adapter weights.

The original DPO method does not require a separately trained reward model. This is not the same as saying there is no reference model. The preference is supplied in the dataset. The fixed reference model is not a judge that first makes this selection, and receives no updates in the documented method. The form described here does not require continual generation of new free responses during optimisation. The later chat does not present and choose from the same pair.

External method references: Rafailov et al. · Direct Preference Optimization and Hugging Face · DPO Trainer and loss function . These explain the general principle; the project experiments used Python/MLX. No use of TRL is claimed.
