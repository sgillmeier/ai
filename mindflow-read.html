Examples, project rules and sources

What this card shows

The card explains the training objective “Ask relevant questions”. All compared questions and suggested responses are editorial illustrations. They are not recorded model responses, new gold-standard texts or evidence of training success. “Suitable” applies to the conversation context shown.

The user prompt under “Clarify a gap” and “Limit the questions” is translated from synthetic probe P4P-06. The four views are not four measurement arms or a controlled single-factor comparison.

The documented basis

02_GUTE_VS_SCHLECHTE_FRAGEN.md describes grounded questions that add information, at most one open question, attention to the situation, and avoidance of generic questions, assumptions, chains of questions and compulsory question endings. 04_VERHALTENSSONDEN_12.md defines twelve synthetic probes, explicitly marked eval-only , with the categories REQUIRED, OPTIONAL and FORBIDDEN. These categories describe target behaviour, not proven success rates.

Historical rules are not universal laws of conversation

The question catalogue excludes “why” questions for this project and gives the historical governor rule: if at least two of the last three companion responses contain a question, the next should have none. This is documented here as a historical target rule, not a scientifically optimal threshold or a verified current app function.

The original documents also refer to “Wärme V2”, acknowledgement, reflection and PPR. These terms remain unchanged in the accompanying originals. The short editorial comparisons here isolate the specific question decision; they are not complete, retrospectively approved gold-standard responses. No new automatic priority logic is constructed from the general question catalogue and the probes. For example, P4P-07 is OPTIONAL in its table, while the explained target response calls for two options directly, without a follow-up question.

Risks: conversation logic, not measured patient harm

Repeatedly asking for known information, asking despite an explicit refusal and assuming unmentioned fear are made understandable through examples. Possible consequences are editorial risk inferences: having to repeat oneself, defend a boundary again or correct a mistaken interpretation. No real user conversations or clinical outcomes were measured for these in the project.

Additional external reference: SAMHSA, TIP 35 (2019), Chapter 3, “The Question-and-Answer Trap”. In a counselling context, the source describes how question-and-answer chains can feel like an interrogation and encourage a passive answering role. This is not evidence of MindFlow’s effectiveness. Only the reasoned conversational risk informs the card design, not a therapy approach.

This card covers ordinary conversational continuations. Necessary safety checks and crisis routing remain separate app tasks. “No questions” here is not a general recommendation to omit necessary clarification when someone is in immediate danger.

What “without assumptions” means here

The response may put what was said in context and explain supported connections. It must not present an unsupported interpretation as fact, assert a feeling that was explicitly denied, or invent a personal relationship with the AI. A plausible-sounding guess, even one introduced with “perhaps”, is not thereby supported by evidence. This does not prohibit every explicitly requested hypothesis: possibilities would have to remain separate from facts and be labelled as such. In these short examples, such a hypothesis is neither needed nor justified.

What SFT means technically here

In the next-token learning described here, the model receives the conversation and the supplied response sequence. At target positions, the probability of the correct next token is evaluated with the preceding target tokens provided (teacher forcing). Cross-entropy, or negative log-likelihood, is calculated from this. The optimiser uses its gradients to change the trainable weights. In the project these were adapter weights; the LoRA structure is explained separately in the technical pipeline.

For F266, the documentation states that only the final assistant turn is a target. Earlier messages provide context; they are not removed from the input merely because they do not count as targets. The animated words are a reading aid, not actual Qwen tokenization or measured probabilities. SFT does not need a preferred/rejected response pair for this signal; the DPO card covers that different signal.

What changes from SFT to DPO

The SFT objective described minimises the negative log probability of the supplied target response. DPO instead uses a context x, a preferred response y+ and a non-preferred response y−. Both responses are scored by the model being trained. A fixed reference model provides the baseline. In simplified terms: m = [log p(y+|x) − log p(y−|x)] − [log p_ref(y+|x) − log p_ref(y−|x)]. The original DPO loss is −log σ(βm).

The relative advantage is intended to grow. This does not mean that the absolute probability of the preferred response must increase at every step, that the poor response becomes impossible, or that a third response is excluded. A better pair score does not guarantee a correct freely generated response. Both methods change trainable weights. In the project experiments described, these were adapter weights.

The original DPO method does not require a separately trained reward model. This is not the same as saying there is no reference model. The preference is supplied in the dataset. The fixed reference model is not a judge that first makes this selection, and receives no updates in the documented method. The form described here does not require continual generation of new free responses during optimisation. The later chat does not present and choose from the same pair.

What else changed in the project

A / A2: Four preference contexts are each used twelve times; 48 positive anchors are added. A2 replaces only the negative CF64-62 sentence. The application example above explains this intervention but is not the historical bed case.

A3: The same A2 pairs, the same order and the same 48 anchors; the positive anchor term receives weight 2 instead of 1. A4: Weight stays at 2; 40 of the 48 anchors remain, and eight are replaced with suitable existing Train266 originals. This is a substitution, not a cost-free extension. The chronicle’s A3 account contains historical results; they are neither recalculated here nor mixed with later summaries that differ.

Additional term: This series added a weighted positive anchor loss to the DPO term. The anchor row usually belonged to a different context from the preference pair. It is not automatically additional SFT on the preferred response of that same pair. The fixed DPO reference and this positive practice serve different purposes.

AN0: The same A4 anchors and their order, but the preference term contributes no training gradient. Pair scores are still measured diagnostically. This is a control arm, not a new complete SFT baseline. The evaluation found local added value from DPO, but did not attribute all losses to DPO alone.

K12: Twelve preference contexts four times each instead of four contexts twelve times each, with 48 updates and 48 A4 anchors unchanged. More contexts and fewer repetitions per context change together. The experiment therefore does not support a blanket claim that diversity inherently helps or harms.

Evaluation approach, project evidence and context

What the examples show — and what they do not

The application message connects to the editorial cards on data development, SFT and DPO. The response alternatives shown here are constructed. “Baseline” and “New version” illustrate an evaluation comparison; they do not attribute these invented texts to historical models. “The acceptance will definitely come” is also a new counterexample. No real user conversations were used, no new gold-standard texts approved and no model responses generated.

Baseline and comparison conditions

The baseline, complete input and generation settings must match the stated comparison. A separately changed prompt or different post-processing must not silently be presented as a training effect. In experiment A (chronicle stage 29), the decoder and complete inputs stayed the same. One continuous training run had separate evaluations after 12, 24 and 48 updates; baseline responses were retained unchanged. The findings card shows the final version A48. It does not claim complete figures for the two earlier checkpoints where these are not supported here.

Free responses are generated without a supplied model answer in the evaluation input. Assessing them separately is different from measuring loss on supplied target tokens. Tasks and evaluation criteria are not freely chosen only after the results are seen. Historical rules remain historical rules: earlier evaluations are not rescored here under later criteria.

A4 / AN0 control experiment

Chronicle stages 35 and 36: AN0 uses the same A4 anchors in the same order; 48 updates and checkpoints 12/24/48 remain. The extra preference term is given coefficient zero; the anchor term retains weight two. Preference pairs and their forward branches remain for technical parity and diagnosis, but contribute no training gradient. “DPO term off” therefore does not mean every corresponding computation disappears. AN0 is not a fresh, complete SFT baseline training run.

The finding is limited: DPO made local additional contributions. It was not the only source of deterioration; positive practice alone was not automatically protective either. A single controlled seed does not establish a general ranking of methods.

Checkpoints, new tasks and retention

Chronicle stages 26 and 29: the 120-case set includes FIT64, NEW16, Replay16 and Contrast24. It is not a fully independent holdout. Learning success on trained FIT cases must be distinguished from transfer to other tasks. A test case once examined during development does not become unknown again by renaming it. Held-out cases must not subsequently be added to the training in this comparison.

The final A version fully completed 84 of 120 tasks, versus 82 at the stated baseline. At the same time, four previously correct protection cases were lost and two new severe error components appeared. Self-correction succeeded from step 12; the bed case retained its contradictory mixed sentence. The judgement was therefore not to adopt it. The diagram’s four crosses show only the number of reported losses; they do not invent individual case identities. Unknown intermediate values are not interpolated.

Assess metrics and freely generated responses separately

For the DPO pilot and in the methods section, the chronicle distinguishes the relative pair score from actual freely generated response quality. A better relative gap can occur even when the preferred response is not reliably generated or a third unsuitable answer appears. The diagram shows no measured probabilities or invented training trajectory. Language quality, loss values and response speed remain different metrics; runtime optimisation belongs in the technical pipeline.

Refine deliberately rather than continue training automatically

Chronicle stage 30: A2 replaced only the negative CF64-62 alternative. The positive target response, other pairs, anchors and hyperparameters remained. 47 reference alternatives stayed byte-identical; the new alternative received its own reference value. The old and new pair scores are therefore not the same unchanged measurement point. Both target tasks were repaired by the end; five previously correct protection cases were lost and three new severe components appeared. No adoption. These figures are historical, not results for the application example on this page.
